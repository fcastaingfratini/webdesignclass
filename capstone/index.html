Alaire is a personal cultural archive — a space to collect writing, cities, reviews, recommendations, and tactile experiments. Built slowly, meant to be returned to.

Read more about the project

This project resulted in a hybrid digital and physical cultural archive that functions as a personal operating system. The core of the project is a website that curates travel-based reflections, recurring rituals, annotated texts, and cultural objects through a slow, editorial structure. Designed to resist rapid consumption and doom-scrolling, the platform reveals limited content per visit and emphasizes revisiting over accumulation. Alongside the website, the project includes a handmade physical object featuring collaged and embroidered imagery, handwritten notes, and printed fragments that do not appear online.

My academic background in New Media and Digital Design, combined with Film and Television studies and a minor in French, as well as my lived experience abroad and Argentine heritage, has shaped my interest in narrative, visual language, and experience design. Outside of coursework, I have consistently worked across digital platforms while maintaining a strong attachment to analog practices such as handwriting, collage, crochet, and plastic arts. Living independently in New York City and spending extended periods in Buenos Aires and Paris further influenced my sensitivity to how place, movement, and routine affect creative thinking.
