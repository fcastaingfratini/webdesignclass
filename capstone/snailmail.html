Process Note

Snail Mail is the physical counterpart to Alaire. If the website is the archive, Snail Mail is the object you can hold — something slower, smaller, and more intimate.

I'm interested in what happens when digital ideas are translated into paper, texture, handwriting, cutouts, printed matter, and collected fragments. The point is not perfection. The point is attention.

Each issue is imagined as a small cultural artifact: part letter, part zine, part archive entry. It may include writing, recommendations, visual references, pieces of cities, film notes, lists, or prompts.

How It's Made

The process begins online — through observation, note-taking, screenshots, saved references, unfinished thoughts, and things I want to return to.

Then it moves offline. Text can be printed and cut. Images can be rearranged. Margins can be annotated by hand. Pages can be stamped, folded, layered, taped, or imperfectly assembled.

I like the idea that the final object still carries evidence of process: marks, textures, edits, hesitation, and touch.

Invitation

Snail Mail is also an invitation to engage more slowly.

To read something without immediately scrolling away.
To make a list by hand.
To recommend something to a friend thoughtfully.
To print something out.
To underline a sentence.
To walk without listening to anything.
To notice what you keep returning to.

I want this page to encourage small rituals of attention — not productivity, not optimization, just slower forms of noticing.

Possible Prompts

You could make your own monthly page of things you loved.
You could write a recommendation letter to a city.
You could collect receipts, movie tickets, wrappers, and notes from a single week.
You could keep a list of what you looked at closely.
You could write down one thought a day by hand.

Collected slowly. Kept with care. Returned to later.
