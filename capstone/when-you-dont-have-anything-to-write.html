When you don’t have anything to write about

June 17th, 2024

About Writing Cities Reviews Recommendations Snail Mail

Hello, world. How are you? It’s been a while. I’ve been to many places, watched many movies, and been inspired by new music and old paintings. Things have been happening inside my little brain. In fact, I’d dare to say they’ve been going on and on. No intention of stopping anytime soon—thank God—or not. So, why haven’t I been, say, writing, painting, or drawing—or doing anything that fucking scratches that persistent itch in my soul to create more and more?

My family always encourages me. They want me to make short films, and some have even offered to fund a small project. I am lucky; I know that. But still, I have not made a film in a hot minute. That’s how you know.

Know what? That art doesn’t care about money. Art doesn’t care about circumstance, people, connections, comfort, success, or money. It doesn’t know how to speak that language. It truly doesn’t give a fuck.

That may be disappointing if you happen to have $1 million sitting in your bank account dedicated to your so-called “passion projects,” because, yeah, art just doesn’t give a fuck.

Inspiration strikes—if it does—when you’re in line at a grocery store rushing to get back home, when you’re filing your taxes, or maybe when you’re at an amazing art show looking at incredible pieces. You can never know. There is no formula. There is no way of knowing when and how this feeling will hit you.

(Right now, I was in the midst of folding my laundry and just felt the urge to open Substack.)

So, why does all of this even matter? If we can’t control this magical feeling of inspiration that just occurs?

Well, my take on it is to be prepared. Act like an artist, a painter, a writer, a doctor—whatever the heck you’re trying to become. Even if you’re not there yet, create a reality that supports and facilitates your objective.

Bring your pen and notebook everywhere you go—even if you only actually use it once out of the two hundred times you went on a walk, and what you wrote was shit. Because you just never know.

The worst thing that can happen is having that magical feeling and it slipping through your fingers like water. Because ideas do that. They knock on your door one day, hoping you’ll take them, and if you don’t, they just continue their path and find another person to actually fulfill them.

Why do you think your wonderful idea that’s been sitting in your mind pantry for years was just done by someone else in a completely different part of the world? Because ideas go where someone will take them. They are looking for their perfect match and, once again, don’t give a shit about you.

So take it as it is. Do as much art as you can. Most of it will be shit, yes. But some of it—may be good.

And as artists, I think that’s all we hope for: that some of our mostly shitty art sticks and is decent.

When you don’t have anything to write about, just write.
