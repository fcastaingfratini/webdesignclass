The Gut Health Craze

March 3rd, 2026

About Writing Cities Reviews Recommendations Snail Mail

When I was 17, my favorite thing to do was blast music with my friends while getting ready to go out together.

We planned these weekends with an almost absurd level of dedication. We’d organize vans to pick us up and take us home, pack food for the ride back, water bottles, blankets, and anything we might need at 3 a.m. after dancing for hours.

The goal was simple: have the most fun for as long as possible.

Now I’m 22. I’m in my last semester of college. And I often find myself deep in YouTube rabbit holes about ancestral diets, doctors explaining gut health, or podcast interviews discussing the world’s stress epidemic.

Don’t get me wrong — I’ve always been curious. I’ve always loved learning. Even at 17 I would fall into internet rabbit holes.

But they were different.

Back then, I might suddenly decide I needed to know everything about Oaxaca, Mexico. Or I’d spend two hours watching a sketchy home-made documentary about how mermaids are real. The curiosity felt random, playful, almost useless in the best way possible.

Now everything seems to orbit around bettering oneself.

Career advice.
Health advice.
Hormonal balance.
Habit stacking.
The dangers of blue light.

And I do find these things interesting. I genuinely care about them. I apply them to my daily life.

But sometimes I wonder: where did playtime go?

Where did learning just for the fun of it go?

Finishing college is a strange moment to exist in.

We’re wrapping up projects we’ve been working on for four years. We’re looking for full-time jobs, applying to grad school, or trying to figure out which city — or which country — we might end up living in a few months from now.

And just when we finally feel comfortable — when we know where every classroom is, how to book a study room, who to ask when we leave our water bottle behind — everything changes again.

There’s something destabilizing about that.

Maybe that’s why so many people in their twenties become obsessed with health and wellness.

Combine that lack of control with an internet full of information, misinformation, endless productivity advice, and a cultural shift away from partying — and you get a generation that is constantly trying to optimize itself.

Is it the lack of control that makes us grip so tightly to the things we can control?

Is our sudden fascination with gut health, sleep cycles, and morning routines just a way of creating stability in an unstable moment of life?

Or are we simply waking up — questioning years of confusing health advice, broken institutions, and systems that suddenly feel less trustworthy than we once thought?

Maybe it’s all of the above.

But sometimes I miss the randomness of curiosity.

The version of myself who would spend an entire afternoon learning something completely useless, simply because it was fascinating.

Not because it would improve my productivity.

Not because it would help my hormones.

Not because it would give me a competitive advantage in the job market.

Just because the world is strange and beautiful and endlessly interesting.

Maybe growing up isn’t about replacing play with optimization.

Maybe the real challenge is remembering that curiosity doesn’t always need a purpose.

Sometimes it’s enough to learn something just because you feel like it.
